# Information Engineering Major Transfer Experience

(Telecommunications Transfer QQ Group: 1037507331)

## 1. Choices

Since you've clicked on this article, I'll get straight to the point:

If you feel you can accept your current major, then you can consider aiming for competitive popular engineering majors (Computer Science, Software Engineering, Information Engineering, Automation, Electrical Engineering, plus Mechatronics). These popular engineering majors generally have very high application-to-admission ratios, fierce competition, and higher risk of failure.

If you absolutely cannot accept your current major and the situation is truly dire, then you should weigh your own circumstances carefully. While popular engineering majors indeed offer better prospects, the failure rate is indeed higher. You can also look at other engineering fields (Energy & Power Engineering, Materials Science, etc.) — first make sure you can get out. Failing to transfer in your freshman year means either transferring in your sophomore year (delayed graduation) or taking a cross-disciplinary postgraduate entrance exam, both of which are more stressful.

Ultimately, how you balance risk and reward is a judgment you'll need to make based on your own situation.

## 3. Specific Preparation

The Information Engineering transfer is divided into a written exam and an interview:

**Written Exam** (around May 7, second semester of freshman year). Requirements as shown:

**Preparation allocation:**

In the first semester of freshman year, the main preparation is to maximize your grades in Advanced Mathematics, Linear Algebra, and overall GPA as much as possible.

During winter break of freshman year, you can appropriately maintain your feel for Advanced Mathematics and Linear Algebra.

In the second semester of freshman year, focus on studying Advanced Mathematics II and College Physics seriously (don't completely neglect other core courses like Probability Theory either, or you'll suffer during finals). Around mid-April, you can start drilling practice problems (College Physics, Linear Algebra, Advanced Mathematics).

Generally, instructors can finish covering the material within the transfer exam scope before the written test, but you may not have enough time for review and practice by then. So it's still recommended to study ahead and leave sufficient time.

**Exam content notes:**

For the 2025 cohort's written exam, Advanced Mathematics and College Physics questions were standard — just practice past papers.

However, Linear Algebra was very difficult, arguably harder than the final exam. There were no routine positive definite matrix problems in the big questions. Instead, there was a proof problem at the end that was very challenging. It's hard to give targeted review strategies — just study normally.

**Other notes:**

The Information Engineering written exam allows calculators. It's recommended to buy a Casio scientific calculator directly — you'll need it for the College Physics final exam too. (Some Linear Algebra and College Physics problems may require a calculator.)

**Interview** (about one week after the written exam):

Requirements as shown:

**Preparation allocation:**

After the written exam, you have less than a week to prepare for the interview. For the self-introduction portion, you can talk about your study methods, any SRP (Student Research Program) projects you've participated in, etc. — write it based on your own situation. (You can mention your CET-4 score and Gaokao provincial ranking — these seem to be frequently asked about.)

The instructors' questions will be based on your self-introduction. At the time, I was asked about my SRP project and some hobbies. The interview is more about your on-the-spot thinking ability. Just relax and answer naturally.
