# Personal Experience Guide & FAQ for Transferring into Mechanical Engineering

2025 Cohort, Mechatronic Engineering — fish

Mechanical Engineering Transfer Group: 238483743

## Introduction to the School of Mechanical Engineering & Personal Journey

SCUT's School of Mechanical Engineering mainly has three majors: Mechatronic Engineering (机电), Mechanical Engineering (机工), and Intelligent Vehicle Engineering (车工). (Safety Engineering, Process Control, and Material Forming & Control are not covered here as I'm not very familiar with them.) Compared to the other two, Mechatronic Engineering includes more electrical-type courses such as Electric Circuits, Digital Electronics, and Analog Electronics, so the coursework load is heavier. If you're aiming for employment after undergrad, choose Mechatronic; if you plan to pursue graduate studies, the differences among the three are minimal. You can check the official website for the curriculum plans. From below CS/Software/Information/Automation, Mechanical Engineering feels like the best transfer choice — welcome to the School of Mechanical and Automotive Engineering!

In the 2025 cohort, because the Academician Class diversion sent a large number of people into Mechanical Engineering (internal diversion within the school), the transfer policy tightened significantly compared to previous years. The requirements were Advanced Math ≥ 90 and English ≥ 85 (a big jump from the 2024 thresholds), which filtered out a lot of people — the number of applicants didn't even exceed the number of available slots. At the time, I felt that CS/Software/Information/Automation was too competitive and I really wanted to escape a dead-end major. Combined with the higher thresholds reducing competition, and the fact that Mechanical Engineering, compared to other majors, offers many competition opportunities (such as RoboMaster, RoboCup, ACM, Formula Student, Intelligent Vehicle, Engineering Training, Electronics Design Contest) with relatively manageable academic pressure — you get more time to compete. In the School of Mechanical Engineering, you can learn practical, hands-on skills and become an engineer. Of course, Mechanical Engineering involves a lot of drafting (you can look up "mechanical engineering drawing" online) and mechanics courses like Mechanics of Materials and Theoretical Mechanics. Before transferring, think carefully about whether you can handle this — some mechanical engineering students have transferred out precisely because they dislike drafting or can't grasp mechanics. In short, the course catch-up burden of transferring is substantial no matter what — it's tough both before and after the transfer. Transferring is a challenge in itself, and daring to take on the challenge is already impressive!

## Transfer Requirements

At the end of April, each school publishes its transfer admission requirements — you can find them on each school's official website. In the 2025 cohort, the School of Mechanical Engineering published theirs relatively late. The documents come out right before the May Day holiday, so I recommend that students planning to transfer use the May Day break to prepare well. Since Mechanical Engineering is a pure interview process (no written exam), the only channels for them to get to know you are your interview PPT and your average GPA. Study hard in the first semester of freshman year, especially for math and English — it's high ROI whether or not the transfer succeeds. If your average GPA isn't very high, I recommend including some mechanical engineering-related professional skills in your PPT, such as SolidWorks and AutoCAD (essential software for mechanical engineering — having these two is pretty much enough). At the time, I self-studied SolidWorks and put a few modeling screenshots in my PPT. You can spend the May Day break learning SolidWorks. Relevant project experience is also fine, and competitions you've participated in (even math competitions or filler contests) can go in too.

Regarding whether liberal arts students face discrimination and whether they can opt for a grade-level downgrade transfer: in the 2025 cohort, Mechatronic Engineering admitted 1 out of 3 liberal arts applicants; Vehicle Engineering had no liberal arts applicants; Mechanical Engineering didn't reject anyone. Overall, if you're a strong liberal arts student, you can absolutely apply for Mechanical Engineering. The academic affairs officer said at the time that whether a downgrade transfer is required would be decided by the school based on credit recognition. In the entire 2025 cohort transfer document, no liberal arts student had a same-year transfer, so liberal arts students hoping to transfer without downgrading should think carefully.

## Transfer Process

After registration closes, you'll receive a phone call notification for the interview around post-May Day. Shortly after, an interview announcement will appear in the academic affairs system (note: not on the school's official website). After joining the Mechanical Engineering transfer interview group, you'll draw lots on the day before the interview to determine your interview order and email your interview PPT to the academic affairs teacher. The interview may start quite early — around 8 or 9 AM. Everyone waits in one classroom, and teachers call people in one by one (somewhat like a number-calling system).

The wait can be excruciating — be prepared. But you can also ask those who went before you what the teachers asked. I was the last one that day. Being later in the order, aside from the longer wait, does help break some information asymmetry. The interview is 5 minutes of self-introduction + 5 minutes of Q&A. There's a timer on screen — don't go over time. Questions are mainly based on the content of your PPT. Don't put random stuff in your PPT, don't exaggerate too much — every detail may be questioned. The teachers are generally kind, though they may ask some tough questions. Don't be nervous, just be confident and natural. It's fine to grill the teachers back (jk). Don't panic if asked whether you can accept a downgrade transfer — engineering majors don't have downgrade transfers.

Attached: Interview PPT Requirements — The personal statement time is 5 minutes, in PPT presentation format. Content mainly includes: personal and family background, academic status, reasons for applying to transfer and understanding of the target major, future academic plans, outstanding potential, hobbies, and special skills, etc. The main assessment criteria are: the student's understanding of the target major, innovative thinking, mastery of foundational public courses, ability to complete the coursework, willingness and ambition to pursue this major in the future, psychological quality, etc.

## Possible Interview Questions

**Mechatronic Engineering questions**: My first question was whether I could accept a downgrade transfer (the tone was very harsh — scared me QAQ). Later they asked about the SolidWorks models in my PPT, the working principle of a reduction gearbox (I knew a bit about this and the teacher seemed quite satisfied), how I did the modeling, and whether I had learned any programming or had any exposure to electrical-related courses.

From other students, questions also included:

1. The role of eigenvectors in mechanical engineering
2. How to prove conservation of mechanical energy (connecting to calculus)
3. What's the difference between digital circuits and analog circuits? Give one example of each in a mechatronic system
4. Differences between Linear Algebra with Analytic Geometry and Calculus
5. University Physics — mechanics and mechanical energy knowledge
6. Is mechanical energy a state quantity?
7. Have you considered other majors?
8. What preparations have you made for transferring into Mechatronic Engineering?

**Mechanical Engineering questions**:

1. My PPT had a hypercube — he asked about it based on my PPT
2. Your original major ranks very highly at SCUT — why do you want to switch to Mechanical Engineering?
3. Have you done any mechanical engineering-related projects?
4. What is linear algebra useful for?
5. Why do you want to transfer to this major?
6. If you transfer, what kind of work do you want to do?

**Vehicle Engineering questions**:

1. Why choose Vehicle Engineering? Why not transfer to Automation?
2. What is the object of study in _Theoretical Mechanics_?
3. During vehicle braking, is it better to brake hard all at once or to pump the brakes?
4. In your original major's studies, what aspects do you think would help with transferring into Intelligent Vehicle Engineering? What connections exist between your original major and Intelligent Vehicle Engineering?
5. During your fleet internship, what kind of work did you do?

## Conclusion

Transferring majors requires immense determination and substantial effort. I hope you keep working hard and stay committed to your goal throughout the first semester of freshman year and the transfer preparation period. Whether you succeed or not, and whichever major you end up in, transferring is an experience of challenging yourself and a tempering process. You can learn a lot from it and reflect on your own shortcomings. Afterward, no matter which major you're in, you'll need to keep grinding. Finally, I wish everyone success in their transfer — welcome to the School of Mechanical and Automotive Engineering!
