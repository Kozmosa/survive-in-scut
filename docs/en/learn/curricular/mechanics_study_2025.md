# Studying Theoretical Mechanics and Mechanics of Materials

By [在路上的小凉](https://github.com/Ryoonmyway)

This account draws on my experience taking these courses in 2025 and is intended as a reference for students in civil engineering, mechanical engineering, and related programs. Some programs combine the two subjects into a course called Engineering Mechanics. Check your own syllabus for the topics covered.

I already had a reasonable grounding in mathematics and physics, and my approach to coursework was mainly focused on exams. For both courses, I concentrated my revision near the end of the semester, working through the lecture slides and repeatedly solving the worked examples and homework problems.

## Theoretical Mechanics

I spent about five days preparing for the Theoretical Mechanics final, studying for one morning each day. My main resources were the lecture slides, their worked examples, and the assigned homework.

I solved the same problems many times. On the second and third passes, I still worked through each problem independently from start to finish. Only after I became comfortable solving them did I switch to reading the questions and recalling the solution methods.

I was prone to calculation errors. When my result was wrong, I compared my work with the solution, found the step where I had gone wrong, and calculated it again. Working through the problems in full on those early passes was important for me. Simply reviewing the method often left calculation errors undiscovered.

Most questions on my final were standard problem types, with methods similar to those used in the slide examples and homework. My overall course grade was 91, which met my expectations. Five mornings was the time I personally spent. Your own revision schedule needs to account for your starting knowledge and the scope of your course.

## Mechanics of Materials

For Mechanics of Materials, I also focused on the lecture slides and repeatedly solved examples and homework problems near the end of the semester. However, when I tried past exam papers, I encountered some problems that I initially did not know how to approach.

One example involved plane stress. The slide examples supplied the normal stress σ and shear stress τ directly. The past exam problem required me to calculate σ and τ from the loading on the structural member before carrying out the plane stress analysis. Practicing the examples from that chapter alone had left those earlier steps unpracticed.

I worked through the solution to understand the entire process. After that, I could solve similar problems independently. Most Mechanics of Materials questions were still fairly direct, so standard problems remained the main focus of my revision.

You can use the slide examples and homework to practice the basic methods, then use past papers to check whether the exam expects you to connect material from different chapters. When you consult a solution for a problem you cannot start, pay attention to where the information needed for each step comes from.

My overall course grade in Mechanics of Materials was 82. However, I sat the exam with a fever of nearly 40°C and was in poor physical condition, which limits how much this grade can tell us about the effectiveness of my revision.
